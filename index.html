1.1 · in review

Who said what — from physics, not guesswork.

Every AI note-taker guesses speakers from voice prints, and every one of them admits it is unreliable. OdiMeet is taking a different route: every old iPhone is a microphone. Put one in front of each participant, let each record, and they merge automatically when you stop.

Each phone hears its own speaker 10–20 dB louder than anyone else (median 12–14 dB in our real tabletop recordings). For every fraction of a second, the loudest track is the person talking. Attribution becomes a measurement, not a prediction — and when two people talk over each other, both are kept.

No hardware to buy. The same trick works with multi-transmitter wireless mics such as the Insta360 Mic Pro or DJI Mic.

FAQ

How is this different from Plaud, Otter or the other AI note-takers?

Four ways. Hardware: they sell you a recorder; OdiMeet uses the iPhone you already have, the voice recorder in your drawer, or the wireless mics you bought for video. Where the audio goes: they upload it to their cloud and meter it by the minute; OdiMeet transcribes on the phone with no account and no meter. Whose AI: they pick the model and bill you monthly; OdiMeet uses your own Gemini key, so you pay Google's actual price and see every request. Who said what: they guess from voice-prints; OdiMeet measures it, by distance or by separate tracks.

Does anything leave my phone?

Audio never does. Transcript text leaves only if you turn on Gemini summaries — and then it goes directly from your phone to Google with your key; there is no OdiMeet server in between. If you also enable Google Search fact-checking, specific claims in the transcript become search queries. Live summaries are on once you add a key and can be turned off in Settings; Search fact-checking is off by default.

Which iPhones work?

Any iPhone that runs iOS 26 — from the iPhone SE (2nd generation) and iPhone 11 up. Apple's engine is comfortable on all of them, and the optional SenseVoice model is fast even on the older ones. A recent iPhone with an OLED screen also makes stealth mode truly black.

What is the lowest-end iPhone you have tested on?

An iPhone SE (2nd generation) with 64 GB, running iOS 26. It is one of our everyday test devices — recording, on-device transcription and the two-phone sync recording all run on it.

What about meetings that mix Chinese and English?

Set the transcription language to Chinese and SenseVoice keeps English words in English inside the Chinese sentence — the right setting for Taiwan-style mixed meetings. Auto-detect listens to the first 30 seconds and picks one language, which suits imported recordings whose language you do not know.

Does it identify speakers?

Yes — and not by guessing. Two iPhones: one in front of each speaker, recording in sync; whichever phone is louder is the person talking, 12–17 dB of separation in our tests, and the transcript is labelled automatically. Wireless lavalier sets (DJI Mic Mini and RØDE Wireless Micro certified): each transmitter is one speaker's track — the most accurate. A single phone can use optional on-device voice-print separation; it is an estimate, confused by similar voices and cross-talk, and the interface says so. In all three you can rename speakers after merging.

Do I still need an external microphone, like a DJI Mic?

For most meetings, no. An iPhone on the table within about half a metre of its speaker records clearly, and a phone per participant is enough for speaker attribution. An external wireless mic earns its place in three situations: noisy rooms or long tables (a lavalier at the collar is 15–20 dB closer to the voice than a phone on the table, roughly twice the separation); when attribution must be near-perfect, as in depositions; and when phones can't sit on a table — walking interviews, field work. If you already own a set, plug its receiver into the iPhone and it works — the ones we have tested and certify are the DJI Mic Mini and the RØDE Wireless Micro (see the next question).

Does it support wireless lavalier mics?

Yes. We have tested the DJI Mic Mini and the RØDE Wireless Micro; both work perfectly: plug the receiver into the iPhone, set the channel mode to Stereo / Split in the maker's app (the only setting you need to touch), and pick "One iPhone + wireless mic set" in New recording — each transmitter becomes one speaker's track, and who said what goes straight into the transcript. Details: DJI Mic Mini, RØDE Wireless Micro. Other receivers that work the same way should too, but we only call a set "certified" once we have tested it.

Are summaries Gemini-only?

Gemini comes first because it is the easiest key to get and the cheapest to run. Amazon Bedrock, OpenAI, Anthropic and custom endpoints are coming — any OpenAI-compatible API will work, including a local model on your own computer such as Ollama. With a local model, transcript text never reaches Google or anyone else.
